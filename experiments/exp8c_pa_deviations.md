# Experiment 8c: PA-filtered deviations as candidate engine misconfigurations

## Hypothesis

Tied to the north star (an instrument that detects eligibility-engine misconfigurations in QC microdata): Pennsylvania's outlier profile — a shelter/excess-shelter error mix distinct from its neighbors (exp8a) — may reflect a PA-specific eligibility-system parameterization, i.e. engine parameters (rates, rounding, caps, deductions, minimums) that differ from the federal schedule. If PA's clean, no-error rows fit the statutory schedule exactly, then the deviations in PA's error rows are caseworker/data issues, not engine misconfigurations. Conversely, systematic deviations on clean rows would be candidate engine misconfigurations worth flagging. This experiment runs the exp8b parameter-fitting battery per fiscal year on PA alone, against the national fleet and the statute, and tests the one known cross-state engine anomaly (uncapped shelter deductions) inside PA.

## Method

Microdata: SNAP QC public-use files FY2020–2024, Pennsylvania (STATE=42), read-only. Two row sets per FY, using the exp8b cleaning rule: clean-engine rows (STATUS=1, RAWBEN==FSBEN, SSI_CAP==0 to exclude PA's PACAP simplified-reporting cases, HOMEDED==1 for housed) and suggestive error rows (STATUS in {2,3}, RAWBEN!=FSBEN, |AMTERR - (FSBEN-RAWBEN)| <= $1), with recoupment and residual-analytics anomalies dropped.

The parameter set is exp8b's, fit per FY on PA clean-engine rows and on the national fleet (Alaska, Hawaii, territories excluded): the 30% benefit-reduction rate (ordinary least squares slope of BENMAX-RAWBEN on RAWNET — ordinary least squares just means the straight line that best fits the cloud of points), the rounding rule for 0.3 x net income (ceiling vs round vs floor exactness), the 20% earned-income disregard, the minimum benefit (modal value — the most common value — in the low-benefit cluster), the standard deduction by household size (fitted from the net-income identity: gross minus earnings minus deductions minus shelter minus net should equal the standard deduction), maximum allotments (median benefit at zero net income), shelter-cap behavior (share of non-elderly cases at or above the cap), and the homeless deduction.

The statutory schedule was compiled from USDA FNS COLA memos, FRAC tables, a CRS report, and state issuing memos (Illinois, Utah, Rhode Island), then cross-validated against the FY2021–2024 technical documentation appendices and against the fleet fits themselves. Three tests: (a) the per-FY PA-vs-fleet-vs-statute parameter table; (b) the exp8b New England shelter-cap anomaly test inside PA (for non-elderly cases with RAWNET>0 and SHELDED above the cap: does the recorded net income match the identity with the shelter deduction capped, or uncapped?); (c) an output-side holdout: predict RAWBEN = max(BENMAX - ceil(0.3 x RAWNET), minimum benefit for 1-2 person households) on PA clean-engine rows and measure exact-match share. A FY2024 SUA spot-check (PA's published HCSUA $750 / LUA $401 / telephone $34) rounds out the input side. Materiality bar: a parameter deviation only counts if it moves benefits by more than $1 for a non-trivial share of cases.

## Results

PA matches the statutory schedule on every parameter, every fiscal year. The 30% rate fits at 0.2999–0.3000 for PA (fleet 0.2968–0.2997), with ceiling rounding exact at 1.000 in all five years. The earned disregard fits at 0.2000 (PA and fleet). Minimum benefits fit at $16/$19/$20/$23/$23 with modal share 1.000 in every year. Standard deductions fit exactly (PA within-$1 share 0.986–1.000). Maximum allotments fit exactly wherever PA has n>=30. The shelter cap binds: PA non-elderly above-cap share is 0.000 in FY2020–2023 and 0.022 in FY2024, which is a single case (details below). On the output side, the statutory formula predicts PA's recorded benefit exactly in 99.7–100% of clean-engine rows (mean absolute error $0.00–$0.16), versus 97.4–98.5% for the fleet. No PA-specific deviation meets the materiality bar.

Two statute-table corrections surfaced during the run; both are fleet-wide, not PA deviations, and the PA-vs-fleet comparison is what caught them:

1. The FY2021 minimum benefit is $19, not $16. The FY2021 file covers only July–September 2021, after the January 2021 15% benefit increase raised the one-person maximum to $234. The FY2021 technical documentation (Table F.6b) lists the January–September 2021 minimum benefit as $19 — 8% of the $234 maximum, rounded. With the wrong $16 floor, the fitted 30% rate looked like 0.21–0.23 for PA and the fleet alike; with the corrected $19 floor it is 0.2999 (PA) and 0.2968 (fleet), and the holdout jumps to 100% exact on PA. The apparent "FY2021 anomaly" was a table error, not an engine behavior.
2. The FY2022 standard deduction is $177/$177/$177/$184/$215/$246 by household size 1–3/4/5/6+, not the Illinois memo's $170/$177/$208/$239. The fleet fits are point masses (mode equals median, n=250–5,425) and a Rhode Island notice corroborates $177/$215/$246. PA fits $177 for sizes 1–3.

The shelter-cap anomaly test finds PA essentially clean. Zero uncapped-shelter clean-engine cases in FY2020–2023; exactly one in FY2024 (SHELDED=$813 against the $672 cap, with net income computed from the uncapped deduction — the exp8b New England pattern); and two suggestive cases in FY2023 error rows, one of which is uncapped-only. PA's engine caps shelter deductions per statute. The fleet background rate is roughly 1–2% of non-elderly shelter cases over the cap.

Input-side checks corroborate: in FY2024, SHELDED=$750 (PA's published HCSUA) appears in 1.0% of PA clean-engine rows, and the top SHELDED values are dominated by $672 (the cap, 99 cases) and $0. PACAP cases (6–16 per FY in PA's cleaned set, excluded from the deduction fits per exp8b) show FY2024 shelter values of 17, 171–218, and 391–414, consistent with the technical documentation's PACAP recording pattern. Proration shows no PA deviation: PA's clean-engine rows contain zero prorated-like cases, but so does the fleet's clean-engine set, and the full-file proration share is 3.6–9.0% for PA versus 5.2–7.8% for the fleet.

## Verdict

Clean PA engine. The parameter-fitting strategy finds no PA-specific misconfiguration: every fitted parameter equals the federal value within $1, the output-side statutory formula predicts PA benefits essentially perfectly, and the one known cross-state engine anomaly (uncapped shelter deductions) is absent from PA. PA's distinctive error mix (exp1/exp8a) is therefore not explained by engine parameterization. This is an informative negative: it rules out the "PA configured its system wrong" hypothesis and redirects the attribution question to the human/process layer — caseworker practice, data capture, or QC review behavior. PA's rows are, if anything, cleaner than the fleet's (holdout exact-match 99.7–100% vs 97.4–98.5%).

Attribution limit: the public-use file identifies states, not eligibility-system vendors, so engine attribution beyond the state level is impossible from this data. Everything here is PA-program behavior.

## Next steps

- The human/process layer is now the live hypothesis for PA's error mix: extend exp8b's unexplained-residual analysis (household-composition and arithmetic residuals) to PA specifically.
- The uncapped-shelter pattern works as a cross-state engine fingerprint: PA is clean, so the follow-up is which states are not, and whether the pattern clusters by known system geography.
- PA's single FY2024 uncapped-shelter case is worth one verification pass against the raw file (a single case can be a recording artifact, not an engine behavior).
- Methodological lesson carried forward: when a sample window straddles a mid-year policy change (the January 2021 15% increase), use the window-specific parameter table (technical-documentation Appendix F), not the October COLA memo. The fleet-comparison design caught both table errors in this run; keep it.
