How the rankers were built

  • One model per jurisdiction from FY2020–2024 federal QC public-use data, using only what the agency has before review (reported income, deductions, rent and utilities, household size, issued benefit).
  • It estimates each case's chance of an error times the likely error dollars, then ranks cases by that.
  • "Lift" is error dollars found in the top 20% (or 10%) of the ranked list, divided by what reviewing the same number of random cases finds, on cases held out from training.
  • "2020–22 → 2023–24" trains on the earlier years and tests on the later ones. "Forecast ÷ actual" compares the model's expected dollars with the dollars found in its top 20%.
  • Review time is assumed to be 30 minutes a case.
  • The five smallest-sample jurisdictions where it helped on both tests (marked ◆) use a model trained on their own cases pooled with every other state's; the rest use their own cases only.
  • Ranking purely by dollars under-reviews elderly households for their risk almost everywhere. The "fairness floor" splits the review budget across household types by their share of errors; it costs a little lift and brings review into proportion.

Cost-share scenarios

From FY2028, states pay part of SNAP benefits if their error rate is 6% or more: 5% of benefits at 6–8%, 10% at 8–10%, 15% at 10% or more. The scenarios estimate how far ranker-guided reviews of active cases could lower the rate. They're the size of the opportunity, not a forecast.

Read with care

  • Small samples make small jurisdictions noisy. Those with fewer than 1,500 QC cases are flagged.
  • The scenarios don't model whether corrections land before the federal sample month, procedural errors outside the public file, or the option to delay the cost share.
  • The ranker orders a state's own reviews. It doesn't touch the federal QC sample or decide anything about a household.
